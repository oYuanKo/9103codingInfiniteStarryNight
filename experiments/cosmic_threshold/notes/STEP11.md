# Cosmic Threshold prototype

Enable/disable in the browser console: cosmicThresholdSettingsV2.enabled = true / false.
Default since Step 11.5: disabled (original portal). No new keys or presentation controls.
See [STEP11_5.md](STEP11_5.md) for isolated A/B/C review and the rejected prototype findings.

Run with Node + Playwright and installed Edge (BROWSER_CHANNEL may override):

    node tests/cosmic.browser.cjs
    node tests/refactor.browser.cjs

The cosmic suite captures matched paused-flow frames at 0, 20, 35, 45, 50, 55,
65, 80, 100 percent under tests/step11-on-*.png and step11-off-*.png.
It tests equal early/end pixels, same-resource reversal, actual wheel pause/reversal,
cache eviction/regeneration and paired frame costs including readback.
An optional ignored step11-original-shader.log from the pre-edit local shader enables
an additional same-browser disabled-versus-original comparison; it is not required
by fresh clones. The archived refactoring JSON fixture is not regenerated here.

Implementation: the original fragment program remains intact. A second program
on the SAME WebGL renderer reconstructs indigo pigment and delays silhouettes
between .35 and .88; the rest uses the original program. Existing brush ownership
and flow provide spatial variation. No extra texture, buffer, new noise or world.
A bounded cache (four entries, now up to 120 curved paths each) samples child sky brush
positions and child flow directions using an independent deterministic hash of both
seeds and the entry star. Canvas 2D paints these sparse cream/gold/blue-grey marks,
with a thinner pigment ridge. They follow the unchanged child camera and gradually
fade into the arriving painting. No elapsed-time simulation, particles or bloom.
The cached paths retain no world/texture references. Regeneration after eviction
is deterministic. Only the portal draw call and shader selection are integrated;
Camera equations, graph, brush replay, micro LOD, hover and group API are unchanged.

Limitations: real curved geometry, but pigment/structure transport is still shader
reconstruction, not one-to-one parent/child stroke morphing. Marks borrow positions
and flow rather than exactly morphing into specific final strokes; fade is visible
if scrutinised. They do not receive the GPU cursor distortion themselves. Baseline
paint still responds to the cursor. Cosmic depth remains an artistic approximation.
PNG/WebM/log outputs are ignored. Tests prove invariants, not subjective naturalness.

## Local validation (Edge, 2026-10-15)

Matched 800x600 paused screenshots were inspected at all nine depths. In this moon
entry, 35% preserves large paint; 50% suppresses child silhouettes and adds sparse
curved pigment; 65% begins returning the child structures. Large parent shapes
remain perceptible. This is an experimental impression, not proof of visual success.

Steady paired frame/readback medians across four alternating batches: disabled
3.6–4.2 ms, enabled 3.6–4.1 ms; batch P95 5.2–8.9 / 6.3–7.5 ms respectively.
No reliable sustained overhead emerged in this run. These are CPU wall times with
GPU readback, not GPU timer queries or guaranteed interactive FPS. Geometry built
in about 0.2 ms (22 paths in the sampled entry). Seven-entry cache pressure ended
with four entries / 96 paths; the hard bound is four entries / 192 paths. Repeated
enabled/disabled draws kept seven renderer textures unchanged. A normal navigation
reset returned to one cached world, four graphics canvases and two textures.

The first experimental shader draw initially took about 152 ms. It now warms on
the first ordinary startup frame using the existing buffer, moving that one-time
compile cost out of the portal. After this change, first experimental portal use
was 6 ms, subsequent use 3.8 ms. Startup has an additional shader draw/compile;
this is not free. Initial shared child texture upload still costs time (the old
portal's first readback was about 51 ms in this measurement). No rendering settings
were changed to hide this cost.

Evidence: step11-on/off-{0,20,35,45,50,55,65,80,100}.png,
step11-comparison.png (left off, right on, depths in the above order),
step11-wheel-roundtrip.webm, step11-cosmic.log and step11-<suite>.log.
The full real-wheel recording is generated by the existing spatial suite and copied
to the Step 11 filename. Original-shader comparison passed at every sampled depth;

other entry positions, multiple levels, Outer return, cursor locality and shortcuts.

variant in brush_flow_shader.js. Navigation, camera equations, startup shader
warmup, early magnified paint, original disabled shader and .88–1 endpoint are
unchanged. Toggle remains cosmicThresholdSettingsV2.enabled.

Twelve sparse groups contain three or four small cached curves each (maximum 48).
Six groups borrow actual child star halo positions; the rest borrow sky brush
positions. Three size/contrast layers suggest distance without parallax or a timer.
Lengths are approximately 2–12 child-world pixels, widths below 1.7, with asymmetric
curvature and occasional cubic subsegments separated by dry gaps. No macro stroke
or new LOD layer is introduced. Existing blue-grey/cream/gold palette is retained.

A small shader adjustment lets warm child pigment regain local structure earlier
than the rest of the composition. It reuses existing flow-aligned pigment samples,
adds no texture lookup/noise field and does not reveal the whole canvas early.

## Step 11.3: painted star river (2026-10-16)

Adds 24 sparse stations with three short curved fragments each to the existing
star/sky groups: at most 120 paths per entry, four LRU entries / 480 paths total.
The previous 48/192 limits are intentionally increased for this bounded geometry;
old reversal and pixel-equality assertions remain intact. No new image, texture,
WebGL buffer or shader program. The same cached Path2D geometry is reused per frame.

Fragments follow a broad curved lane in child-world sky coordinates, dispersed
across its width with seeded variation in size, contrast and curvature. Local
child flow bends their orientation; existing groups still anticipate actual child
star halos. A matching soft pigment current in the experimental shader gives
selective tonal depth without a solid painted strip, bloom or extra noise field.
The middle-stage colour reconstruction suppresses old large silhouettes more
strongly and attenuates their displaced relief; a trace remains, while the small
curves provide detail. This only affects .35–.88. The early magnified phase,
normal endpoint, original disabled shader, camera equations and navigation are
unchanged. Progress, seeds and child positions fully determine the new composition.

Validation: cosmic, cosmic_enrichment, navigation, portal, spatial, hierarchy,
hover_detail, app and refactor browser suites. Enrichment checks three seeds and
two entries, exact pixels on cache regeneration and reverse, early/final equality,
curve bounds and the distributed sky lane. Real wheel pauses/reversals at .4/.5/.6
are covered by cosmic; spatial records a full wheel in/out; navigation covers
multiple levels and Outer return.

Evidence: step113-comparison.png (left disabled, right enabled; rows 20/35/45/50/
55/65/80/100 percent), step113-seeds.png (columns seeds 12/89/205; rows moon and
major-1 at 50%), individual step113-<seed>-<star>-on/off-<depth>.png,

## Step 11.4: Galaxy Interior and Proto-Universe (validated 2026-10-17)

These are overlapping visual weights inside the existing portal, not navigation
states, world nodes or cameras. Runtime changes are in cosmic_threshold_v2.js and
brush_flow_shader.js; nested_universe_v2.js gains only a geometry-cache release
call when a world is disposed. App orchestration, integration API, camera equations,
world seeds, brush generation and LOD are unchanged. Earlier uncommitted work is
retained. No commit/push is performed.

### Developer comparisons

All settings live in cosmicThresholdSettingsV2; no public UI or new keys:

- enabled = false: original Step 6D.3 renderer.
- enabled = true, galaxyInterior = false, protoUniverse = false: Step 11.3.
- enabled = true, galaxyInterior = true, protoUniverse = true: full Step 11.4 (default).
- Either stage may be disabled independently. Flags change the visual treatment
  immediately; they are intended for paused A/B review, not an animated UI toggle.

### Weights and correspondence

Galaxy Interior rises smoothly from .35 to .42 and retreats from .50 to .68.
Proto-Universe rises from .48 to .62 and retreats from .72 to .88. Their sum is
clamped for fragment opacity; no clock controls progression. Formation coordinates
use smoothstep(.48,.72,p). The accepted magnified-paint phase through .35 is kept
exactly, slightly longer than the conceptual .30 start in the brief. At .35 the
weight is zero; .40 shows the emerging interior; .50 is the strongest galaxy view.
By .60 actual Child stars establish; by .70 sky/terrain structure is resolving;
all extra influence ends by .88. The .0/1 endpoints use the original shader.

The interior reconstructs deep pigment around forked soft currents and clouds
informed by the Child Flow Map and six actual major-star/moon positions. It takes
low-amplitude fine pigment differences from a sky-only portion of the existing
Child painting to avoid revealing village/cypress silhouettes during the galaxy
stage. Parent macro relief and full Child contrast are suppressed there. The
proto stage uses the destination's star positions/radii to establish local contrast
before the terrain, while the existing Child flow and colour become clearer.

No extra brush paths are added: the existing <=120 cached paths acquire galaxy
positions and destination halo positions (0.8–2.3 actual star radii), plus local
flow directions. Positions/orientations interpolate deterministically; paths do
not globally shrink. In the default combined mode their placement stays at the
formed destination while opacity fades, rather than returning to the star river.
The four-entry/480-path bound is unchanged. Cache entries contain numbers/paths,
not world or texture references, and are removed when associated worlds dispose;
seed reset was tested to clear the cache.

The previous cosmic shader remains intact as a separate compiled variant, so
turning both new flags off exactly recovers Step 11.3. One additional shader
program is prewarmed in the existing renderer at startup. There are no additional
textures, fullscreen buffers, runtime readbacks, painting rebuilds or world nodes.
Two existing-paint samples and six bounded star-density evaluations are added to
the enhanced fragment path. The extra program has a startup compilation cost;
that total startup cost was not separately measured.

## Continuity correction after visual review (2026-10-17)

This section supersedes the Step 11.4 stage timing and long-distance geometry
placement described above. The user found the transition unnatural. Inspection
confirmed a rapid full-screen darkening at .35-.42, followed by simultaneous
star recovery as the galaxy suppression ended. Passing navigation/pixel tests
had not established visual acceptance.

The enhanced shader now derives directly from the unchanged base portal instead
of applying both the old threshold and a second strong reconstruction. The prior
Step 11.3 A/B program is retained verbatim. Galaxy influence rises over .35-.55
and recedes over .55-.84; proto influence rises over .40-.68 and fades .74-.88.
The galaxy modifies at most 70% of the pigment carrier, retaining paint texture.
Parent contrast retirement is monotonic and independent of the fading galaxy
weight, so old silhouettes cannot return behind the new stars. Existing local
structure arrivals are broadened by a quadratic contrast response rather than
being suppressed to zero across the whole image. Child flow interpolation,
Camera, world graph, original painting and all LOD generation are unchanged.

Temporary curves keep their original child-space anchors. Halo-directed gathering
is limited to 12 world units over the entire transition, instead of flying from
the river to distant star centres. Fade-in is spread over .22 depth, with smooth
fade-out ending by .88. Same paths/count/cache; no extra textures or buffers.
This sacrifices some of the distinct dark-galaxy scene in favour of continuity.
It remains shader pigment/contrast reconstruction, not matched stroke morphing.
